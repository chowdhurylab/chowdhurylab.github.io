Using CatLog

CatLog lists enzyme kinetic constants (kcat, Km, kcat/Km), each with its enzyme, organism, substrate, assay conditions and source paper. It is maintained by the Chowdhury Lab at Iowa State University.

The CatRange paper (PNAS Nexus, 2026) describes CatLog-27k, 27,176 kinetic records curated from BRENDA and SABIO-RK on which its models were trained; this site is the growing catalog that followed.

How this catalog is built: see How CatLog reads and checks values.

How CatLog reads and checks values

Read from the paper
CatLog reads papers directly. An AI model (Claude, Anthropic) reads each kcat, Km and kcat/Km with its unit, enzyme, organism, substrate and assay conditions. A second, separate AI check compares every value with the paper. Code confirms that the quoted line is in the paper text, and converts units where the paper’s differ from the catalog’s. Each value keeps where it is printed and the number as printed. A person has not checked these values unless the record is marked human-checked.
AI audits
A separate AI auditor re-checks sampled values against their papers and tries to find errors; values found wrong are removed.
Human review
Human reviewers compared values with their papers in a review of the earlier catalog. Accepted records where a reviewer approved every value carry human-checked.
Earlier catalog
The other records come from BRENDA, SABIO-RK, the Open Enzyme Database, UniProt, SKiD and STRENDA DB, and from earlier paper extractions, screened by an earlier automated pipeline of AI models and code checks. When CatLog reads a paper, its records replace that paper’s earlier ones.
Who does it
People and AI agents work in a loop: what people find in the checks improves how the next papers are read. See People behind CatLog.

Find records

Type an enzyme, EC number, organism, substrate, UniProt accession, PMID or DOI in the search box: laccase, 1.10.3.2, P11509, 12514023. Several words narrow the search (Trametes laccase); common names such as human, mouse, yeast or E. coli find the species, and β-lactamase finds beta-lactamase. Gene names are not indexed.

Filters
Source (read from the paper by CatLog or from enzyme databases); review result, with human-checked records; enzyme, organism, substrate and EC number (1.1.1 lists the whole subclass); measurement type (kcat, Km, kcat/Km); wild type or variant; temperature and pH range; cautions.
Sort
By review result (the default), EC number, name or value. A click on a value’s column header sorts high to low, a second click low to high; records without the value stay last.
Share
A filtered view has its own address: copy it from the address bar. So does an open record: the address adds record= and the record ID to the filters.

Example views

  • Human-checked records #browse?checked=1
  • EC 1.1.1, wild type, measured at 25–37 °C #browse?ec=1.1.1&variant=wild&tmin=25&tmax=37
  • Accepted kcat/Km values without a caution, highest first #browse?status=accepted&metric=kcat_over_km&caution=none&sort=kcat_over_km

Read a record

Click a record in the table, or press Enter on it, to open it.

Header
The enzyme name; EC number, organism and form (wild type or the variant); the substrate; the status line, the review result and its tags, for example Accepted · read from the paper · human-checked; and the record ID with Copy link.
Values
Each value in catalog units with its unit and any caution. A record read by CatLog also gives each number as printed in the paper and where it is printed.
Source
Where the values come from, how they were read and checked, the license and the snapshot’s data ID.
Conditions
Temperature, pH, the other assay conditions, cosubstrate and preparation.
Protein and substrate
Wild type or variant, UniProt accession, protein sequence, substrate and SMILES, where CatLog has them.
Paper
The title, PubMed and DOI links, and where in the paper the values are printed.
Against the databases
Where the snapshot has it: how the values compare with the numbers the enzyme databases list for the same paper, or what an earlier review changed. A database record not yet checked against its paper says so.
Review and audit
The review result in one sentence, any AI audit and what a human reviewer approved; then Download record and Report a problem.
Source details
Where the enzyme name comes from and which databases a merged record combines.

Cautions

An amber beside a value flags a possible problem; open the record to read what it is. On phones the short words below stand beside it. A value without a recorded unit shows no unit, and a questionable temperature has the mark in the T column, after a dash when the temperature is not shown.

kcat/Km mismatch
The kcat/Km printed in the paper (or listed by the source) and kcat ÷ Km differ. The record gives both numbers and how far apart they are; a factor near 60 usually means minutes against seconds, and one near 1000 a concentration unit. All three values are kept as the source gives them.
Km > 10 M, Km < 1 nM
Km above 10 M or below 1 nM; the unit or the value may be wrong.
kcat > 108 s-1
kcat above 108 s-1; the unit or the value may be wrong. The fastest enzymes measured, such as catalase and carbonic anhydrase, reach 1–3 × 107 s-1.
possible 1000× unit error
The stored kcat/Km may be 1000-fold off through a concentration unit; where the source lists a kcat/Km that agrees with kcat ÷ Km, the record shows that one.
above diffusion limit
kcat/Km above the diffusion limit; the unit or the value may be wrong.
no unit
The source gives no unit, so the column unit does not apply.
printed unit differs
The paper prints the value in another concentration unit than the one CatLog read it in (µM printed, mM used). The record says whether kcat ÷ Km shows the unit used is right and, when an AI audit re-checked the value, what it found. When the snapshot carries this caution with the record, the table and the Cautions filter count it under Possible unit or magnitude error; otherwise it is shown in the record only.
T questionable
The stored temperature or its unit is questionable, for example exactly 0 °C, which may stand for a missing value; the record’s note says which.

The Cautions filter groups them as kcat/Km mismatch, Possible unit or magnitude error (Km > 10 M or < 1 nM, kcat > 108 s-1, possible 1000× unit error, above diffusion limit, printed unit differs), No unit given and Temperature questionable; No cautions finds the records with none of these.

Review result

Each record has one review result and may carry tags.

Accepted
Passed review: read from the paper by CatLog, or accepted by the earlier automated review as printed or after a correction.
Not confirmed
The earlier automated review left a question open on the value, unit, protein or substrate. The record is replaced when CatLog reads its paper.
Not checked
No review result. Unverified: the earlier review recorded no final check. Not reviewed: a database record without a PMID or DOI (SKiD or BRENDA) that was never reviewed. The record shows which, for example Not checked (Unverified).
Disputed
A check found a problem; not accepted.

Tags

from paper
Read from the paper by CatLog (see How CatLog reads and checks values).
earlier extraction
Read from a paper by the earlier automated pipeline of AI models and code checks, not by CatLog.
corrected
Accepted after a correction in the earlier automated review; the record shows the changes when the snapshot lists them.
no paper linked
Accepted, but no PMID or DOI is linked, so the value cannot be traced to a paper.
human-checked
A human reviewer approved every value on the record (on a record read by CatLog: the same values in the earlier catalog). Shown on accepted records only; most records have not been checked by a person.
Evidence labels on older records
Located in the paper
The earlier automated review saved where the paper prints the values; the text may describe context rather than this value. This alone does not mean Accepted.
Note from BRENDA
The record shows the source database’s own note under this heading; no paper text is saved.
Several database records
The record merges several database entries; no paper text is saved.
Paper cited
On an accepted record: a PMID or DOI is linked and the values were checked; where the paper prints them was not saved.
Paper cited (values not located)
On a record that is not accepted: a PMID or DOI is linked; where the paper prints the values was not saved.
Database record only
Only the source database record is saved.

Units

kcat
Turnover number, s-1.
Km
Michaelis constant, mM.
kcat/Km
Catalytic efficiency, s-1 mM-1 (multiply by 1,000 for M-1 s-1).
T (°C), pH
Assay temperature and pH; the record lists the other conditions.

On a record read by CatLog, code converts each printed value to these units (µM to mM, min-1 to s-1). Stored values drop trailing zeros (a printed 0.050 mM is stored as 0.05): the table and the downloads give the stored number, and the record’s values table keeps the zeros the paper prints (0.050) beside the value as printed.

calc.
A kcat/Km CatLog worked out as kcat ÷ Km; the table and the record show it with the significant figures of the less precise of the two (at least two). A record read from the paper has one only when the paper prints none. An older record’s source may still list a kcat/Km of its own, which the record shows.
Printed kcat/Km
Kept as printed, also when it differs from kcat ÷ Km; the record then gives both (kcat/Km mismatch).
Other units
A few older records keep another unit, shown after the value (min-1, mg/mL), or have no unit; those numbers are not in the column unit.
Large and small
Numbers of 100,000 or more and below 0.001 are written as a × 10n (8.2 × 10-4).
Digits
The table and the record show at most 4 significant figures, and 3 where a conversion from per minute left repeating digits (30.333 shows as 30.3); a whole number keeps all its digits. The record keeps a zero the paper prints after the decimal point (a printed 135.0 s-1 reads 135.0 there and 135 in the table) but never shows more figures than the paper prints (a printed 47,000 M-1 s-1 reads 47 s-1 mM-1, not 47.00). The tooltip and the downloads keep the stored digits.
Missing values
A dash, used only in table cells, means no value (in the T column, a temperature flagged as questionable). In the record a kcat, Km or kcat/Km that is not stored has no row, and the Values section says when none is stored. Any other detail the paper or the source database does not give reads Not stated in the paper (records read by CatLog) or Not recorded (database records); a missing PMID or DOI, sequence or SMILES reads None, or says why there is no sequence.

Before you use the values

Review result
Only Accepted records passed review: Not confirmed records have an open question and Not checked records have no review result. Most records have not been checked by a person.
Units
Read the unit beside each value: a few older records keep another unit or none (see Units).
kcat/Km
See the caution kcat/Km mismatch.
Merged records
Some older records merge several database entries; the record says how many and lists their papers.
Training sets
Records of one paper share its PMID: split training and test sets by paper or by UniProt accession.

Cite this snapshot

Cite the paper and the snapshot you used:

Sajeevan, K. A., et al. (2026). CatRange enables robust prediction of enzyme variant kinetic regimes. PNAS Nexus, pgag309. doi: 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag309.

CatLog snapshot (date and data ID appear when the catalog loads)

For one value, add its CatLog record ID (at the top of the record); search finds it. IDs of records read by CatLog can change in later snapshots.

Keep the file you used: older snapshots leave this site over time.

If you use records from a database, cite that database as it asks: BRENDA, SABIO-RK, SKiD (Zenodo 15355031), the Open Enzyme Database or UniProt (links under Sources and licenses).

Questions

Why does a value differ from BRENDA?

A value read from the paper by CatLog is converted by code where needed, so it can differ from a database entry for the same paper: the database may give it in another unit, round it or take it from another table, assay condition or enzyme form. The record shows the value as printed and where it is printed. Older records keep their source database’s value.

Why is a sequence or SMILES missing?

CatLog adds a sequence only when it can match the enzyme to a protein entry, and a SMILES only when the substrate name resolves to one structure; otherwise the field stays empty rather than guessed, and the record says so or gives a sequence note. A variant gets its own sequence only where its mutations could be placed; otherwise the record gives the wild-type reference.

What does Not confirmed mean?

The earlier automated review left a question open on the value, unit, protein or substrate, so the record is not accepted. It is replaced when CatLog reads its paper. Use its values with that in mind, or filter to Accepted records.

How do I report an error?

Open the record and choose Report a problem: it starts an email to the lab with the record ID and the snapshot. For anything else, use Report a data issue.

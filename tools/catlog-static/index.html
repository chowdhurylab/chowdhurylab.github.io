People behind CatLog

Using CatLog

CatLog re-verifies published enzyme kinetics: kcat, Km and kcat/Km. AI agents find the papers that enzyme databases cite, read the values in them and check each against the paper; people review samples.

By the Chowdhury Lab, Iowa State University. CatLog grew out of CatLog-27k, the 27,176 BRENDA and SABIO-RK records the CatRange paper (PNAS Nexus, 2026) trained its models on.

How CatLog works

How CatLog reads a value

  1. Find the paper The paper a database value cites, by its PMID or DOI.
  2. Read An AI model (Claude, Anthropic) reads each value with its unit, conditions and the line it is printed in.
  3. Check A second, separate AI check compares every value with the paper. Automated checks confirm the line is in the paper’s text.
  4. Compare Each value is compared automatically with the numbers the enzyme databases list for the same paper.
  • As printed The record keeps the number, its ± and unit as the paper prints them, and where (Table 1).
  • Units The printed unit is converted automatically (9.2 µM becomes 0.0092 mM); no model types a converted number.

Then checked again. AI audits re-check samples against the papers and people review samples; what they find improves how the next papers are read. Checks so far on Stats People behind CatLog

Labels and colours

Each colour means one thing, and its word always stands beside it.

Review result

Accepted
Passed review: read from the paper by CatLog or accepted by the earlier automated review (as printed or after a correction).
Not confirmed
The earlier review left a question open; replaced when CatLog reads the paper.
Not checked
No review result. The record names which kind: Unverified (the earlier review recorded no final check) or Not reviewed (a SKiD or BRENDA record with no PMID or DOI that was never reviewed).
Disputed
A check found a problem; not accepted.

Tags

from paper
Read from the paper by CatLog’s current, second version (how).
not re-read
From CatLog’s earlier version, which started from database entries; its source follows. When CatLog reads the paper, its records replace these.
earlier extraction
Read from a paper by CatLog’s earlier version (AI models and automated checks).
corrected
Accepted after an earlier correction; the record shows the change.
no paper linked
Accepted, but no PMID or DOI is linked, so the value cannot be traced to a paper.
human-checked
A reviewer approved every kcat and Km shown; the kcat/Km too, or it agrees with kcat ÷ Km. Accepted records only.

Cautions

An amber exclamation mark beside a value flags a possible problem; the record says what it is. For the unit and magnitude cautions, the unit or the value may be wrong. The Cautions filter, under More filters, finds them by group; No cautions finds records with none.

Before you use the values

  • Review result. Only Accepted records passed review; most records have not been checked by a person.
  • Units. Read the unit beside each value (Units).
  • kcat/Km. Mind the caution kcat/Km mismatch.
  • Merged records. Some records not yet re-read merge several database records; the record lists their papers.
  • Training sets. A paper’s records share its PMID: split by paper or by UniProt accession.

Find records

Search, filters and shareable addresses
Search
An enzyme, EC number, organism, substrate, UniProt accession, PMID or DOI: laccase 1.10.3.2 P11509 12514023. More words narrow it; human or E. coli finds the species; gene names are not indexed.
Filters
Enzyme, EC number (3 or 1.1.1 lists everything under it), organism, substrate; measurement type; review result, with human-checked; source: read from the paper or not yet re-read. A group’s ? says what its options mean.
More filters
Wild type or variant, assay conditions (temperature, pH), against the databases (how a record’s values compare), cautions and source database. It opens by itself when one of them is set; its title counts them.
Sort
By review result (accepted first), EC number, name or value; click a value’s header for high to low.
Share
Every view and open record has its own address: copy it from the address bar.

Try

  • Human-checked records
  • EC 1.1.1, wild type, 25–37 °C
  • Accepted kcat/Km without a caution, highest first

Units

kcat s−1 · Km mM · kcat/Km mM−1 s−1
  • kcats−1Turnover number
  • KmmMMichaelis constant
  • kcat/KmmM−1 s−1Catalytic efficiency (× 1,000 for M−1 s−1)
Conversion
Each printed value is converted automatically to these units (µM to mM, min−1 to s−1).
calc.
A kcat/Km CatLog worked out as kcat ÷ Km, to the precision of the less precise of the two; on a record read from the paper only where CatLog read no printed kcat/Km.
Printed kcat/Km
Kept as printed, also when it differs from kcat ÷ Km (mismatch).
× 10n
Numbers of 100,000 or more and below 0.001: 8.2 × 10−4.
Digits
A value read from the paper keeps the paper’s significant figures, at least 2 (135.0 stays 135.0); a unit conversion keeps one more where rounding would shift the printed last digit (87.3 min−1 is 1.455 s−1). Records not yet re-read: at most 4 (3 after a per-minute conversion). The downloads keep the stored digits.
Ki, T, pH
Ki in mM, on the few records whose source lists one; temperature (°C) and pH with the record’s conditions, in More filters (Assay conditions) and in the downloads (the table has no column for them and shows them only to tell apart records of one paper).
Missing
A dash, used only in table cells, means no value. In a record, a kcat, Km or kcat/Km that is not stored has no row, and the Values section says when none is stored; another missing detail reads Not stated in the paper (read by CatLog) or Not recorded (database records), and a missing PMID or DOI, sequence or SMILES reads None.
Other units
A few records not yet re-read keep another unit (min−1, mg/mL) or no unit; those numbers are not in the column unit.

Questions

Why does a value differ from BRENDA?

The database may give it in another unit, round it or take it from another table, condition or enzyme form; the record shows the value as printed and where. Records not yet re-read keep their database’s value. Against the databases says how CatLog compares them.

Why is a sequence or SMILES missing?

CatLog adds a sequence only when the enzyme matches one protein entry, and a SMILES only when the substrate name resolves to one structure; otherwise the field stays empty rather than guessed. A variant gets its own sequence only where its mutations could be placed.

What does Not confirmed mean?

The earlier automated review left a question open on the value, unit, protein or substrate, so the record is not accepted; it is replaced when CatLog reads its paper. Filter to Accepted records to leave such records out.

How do I report an error?

Open the record and choose Report a problem: it starts an email to the lab with the record ID and the snapshot. For anything else, use Report a data issue.
